## Team
Led by Will Lawrence, who brings top-tier domain expertise from leading Facebook’s AML product and building compliance infrastructure at Paxos. This is a founder with deep 'insider' knowledge of the exact workflows being automated.

## Product
A workforce of AI agents that handle routine AML and KYC tasks. Distinguishes itself via 'Trust Infrastructure'—a proprietary system for model validation and auditability designed to satisfy Fed, FDIC, and OCC regulations.

## Market
Large and high-stakes. Financial institutions face massive compliance costs and regulatory pressure. The shift toward 'AI-native operations' in the back office is a timely response to the limitations of human-only compliance teams.

## Traction
Strong institutional backing and capital. Raised a $4.8M Seed followed by a $15M Series A (reported May 2025). The transition from Bretton AI to the Greenlite brand indicates scaling momentum.

## Competitive Advantage
The 'Trust Infrastructure' is the moat. While generic AI agents are easy to build, agents that are 'audit-ready' and cited for regulated bank workflows are much harder to replicate within 3 months, as they require deep regulatory alignment.

## Thesis Fit
Poor. While the product is 'autonomous' (doing the work), it fails the '15-minute self-serve/no IT' requirement. Selling to FDIC-regulated banks and integrating with 'their own data and policies' invariably requires security reviews, IT involvement, and lengthy sales cycles.

## Risks
The primary risk is the 'Death by Pilot'—the long lead times required to integrate with legacy bank systems and pass security audits. Additionally, regulatory bodies may change requirements for AI-driven audits faster than the startup can adapt.

## Confidence
High

## What Would Change My Mind
If they released a 'Greenlite Lite' version that allowed a fintech to upload a CSV of flagged transactions and get an automated AML report in 5 minutes via credit card payment, it would meet the self-serve thesis. Currently, it's a heavy enterprise sale.
