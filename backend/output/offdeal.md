## Team
Founded by Ori Eldarov (CEO) and Alston Lin (CTO). Eldarov brings domain expertise from Harvard Business School and finance, having experienced the pain of SMB acquisition personally. Lin provides technical depth with a background at Facebook and Google focusing on big data and ranking engines.

## Product
An AI broker that automates the SMB acquisition lifecycle. It features a semantic search engine to screen millions of businesses, web-enabled AI agents for automated diligence, and an automated outreach system that crafts hyper-personalized messages to business owners.

## Market
Initial target is the 'search fund' market (MBA-led acquisition vehicles) which has a strict 2-year deployment window. Expansion plans include private equity firms and the broader 'acquisition entrepreneurship' demographic. Targets businesses in the $10-50M range.

## Traction
Closed beta launched two weeks prior to the source date with approximately 12 buyers currently using the platform to source acquisitions in the $10-50M range.

## Competitive Advantage
Proprietary recommendation engines and custom-built AI agents designed to replace the manual work of offshore assistants and interns. The advantage lies in the speed (minutes vs. days) and scale (thousands of companies vs. ~150 per week) of the search and vetting process.

## Thesis Fit
Strong fit on autonomy: the product 'does the work' of a deal team/interns rather than just assisting. High fit on setup: semantic search allows non-technical buyers (MBAs/investors) to define criteria and generate lists immediately. Weakness in defensibility: the core value proposition relies on LLM-powered search and agents, which well-resourced incumbents like Grata or Sourcescrub could potentially replicate quickly.

## Risks
Low defensibility against incumbent databases; market size of search funds is niche; AI-generated outreach faces increasing deliverability and 'spam' filters; accuracy of web-agent diligence for private SMB data which is often not public.

## Confidence
Medium

## What Would Change My Mind
I would move to a 'High' confidence 'Yes' if they could prove a data moat (access to non-public financial data) that incumbents cannot replicate, or if they demonstrate a high 'success-to-close' rate that proves their AI identifies better deals than manual search.
