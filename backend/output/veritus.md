## Team
Exceptionally strong founder-market fit. Joshua March is a serial founder with a successful SaaS exit to a public company (Verint) and experience with a16z-backed ventures. David Schlesinger and the engineering lead (Joey) bring core fintech infrastructure experience from Divvy Homes, which is critical for navigating the complexities of loan servicing.

## Product
An omni-channel platform deploying autonomous AI agents for consumer lending. Unlike simple chatbots, these agents negotiate repayment plans and handle inbound/outbound voice calls. The product focuses on vertical specialization and regulatory compliance to drive recoveries with 'zero human involvement.'

## Market
The consumer debt collection and loan servicing market is massive and notoriously inefficient. It is 'investable now' because recent breakthroughs in voice AI allow for the high-fidelity, empathetic, and legally compliant communication required for debt negotiation, which was previously impossible for software to handle autonomously.

## Traction
Selected for Y Combinator (Summer 2025). The company is currently in the 'design partner' phase, seeking lenders and agencies to pilot the platform. No specific revenue or recovery data is provided.

## Competitive Advantage
Their moat is not just the AI, but the regulatory 'hard work.' By becoming a fully licensed collections agency in all 50 US states and buying debt themselves, they create a significant barrier to entry. This regulatory stack cannot be replicated by a well-resourced competitor within 3 months, as state licensing is a slow, bureaucratic process.

## Thesis Fit
Mixed. The product excels at the 'autonomous value' pillar (it negotiates and recovers debt rather than just assisting). However, it likely fails the '15-minute self-serve/no IT' pillar. Debt collection and loan servicing typically require deep integration with a lender's Loan Management System (LMS) and sensitive PII data, making 'no IT involvement' highly improbable in this vertical.

## Risks
Regulatory risk is paramount; a single AI 'hallucination' that violates the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) could lead to massive fines or loss of licenses. Integration friction may also prevent the rapid, self-serve growth our thesis requires.

## Confidence
High

## What Would Change My Mind
To move to a 'Yes,' I would need evidence that a lender can start recovering debt through Veritus (e.g., by uploading a simple CSV of charged-off accounts) without requiring a custom API integration into their core banking systems. I would also need to see if their 'licensed agency' model allows them to bypass the long sales cycles typical of fintech enterprise software.
